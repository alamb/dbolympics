Priyanka,

Good work — this is exactly the kind of digging I wanted. A few things surprised me and now matter more because of the regulator inquiry (which I'm handling jointly with Henrik Aalto, CISO — our response needs to be accurate and defensible, not just reassuring).

What struck me:

- The three "open" erasure requests aren't really open, they're parked behind legal holds — but we don't actually know if those holds are still justified. If a regulator asked us today why those erasures haven't happened, "disputes" that are 1.5–2.6 years old is not a comfortable answer without evidence.
- We have a closure-tracking gap (rejected requests never get a completion date) and apparently zero portability requests in three years. Both could be true, or both could be an artifact of how we log things — and I don't want to hand the regulator a number I can't stand behind.
- The email retention failure is concrete and fixable, but it also means I can't yet answer "what remains after an account is deleted" with confidence for other categories until we know how widespread this is.
- The consent anomaly (one category showing suspiciously perfect opt-in) undermines my confidence in saying "this is how consent is recorded" to a regulator until it's explained.

So, for this week, please prioritize work that lets us answer the DPA's three questions honestly: volume and speed of privacy requests, how consent is captured and whether it's reliable, and what data persists post-deletion. That's the deliverable Henrik and I need to brief the board audit committee before we respond.

My questions:

1. For the three long-running legal holds, can we establish — from whatever evidence exists — whether the underlying disputes are still active, so I can decide whether to push for release or document why they must stand?
2. Can we get a trustworthy, defensible count of privacy requests received and resolved, including the rejected ones and whether "zero portability requests ever" is real or a logging blind spot — the regulator will ask for exactly this?
3. How confident can we be in what consent records actually show, given the anomaly you flagged — do we have other categories with the same too-clean pattern, and does that change how I'd describe our consent process to a regulator?
4. Beyond email, how much shopper data elsewhere is sitting past its stated retention window right now — I need a true current picture of "what remains after deletion," not just the one confirmed violation.

If all four can't fit, start with 2 and 4 — those go directly into the regulator response.
