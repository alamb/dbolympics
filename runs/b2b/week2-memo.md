**Memo: B2B Wholesale Pipeline — Follow-Up Findings**
*From Daniel Kim, Fri (week 2)*

**Q1 — Lost deals are actively fought, not neglected.** Only 40% of lost opportunities have logged activity in their first quarter of life, but that jumps to 100% in the second quarter and stays high through the third (96.7%) and fourth (76.7%) (*Lost Deals: Activity Timing*, night 1). Reading: these aren't deals that go cold and get forgotten — the large majority are still being worked right up near the end. But ~23% *do* go quiet before closing, a real minority worth flagging separately as true "stalled and abandoned" cases. Confidence: high.

**Q3 — Youssef Meijer loses his biggest deals, and it shows at the account level too.** His 7 deals span 7 distinct accounts — no territory concentration. But his 3 losses include a €26,000 deal, so while his count-based win rate (57.1%) looks only moderately below average, his **dollar-weighted win rate is 25.7%** — he's keeping barely a quarter of the pipeline value he touches (*Youssef Meijer: Individual Opportunities*; *Dollar-Weighted Win Rate by Rep*, night 2). I checked whether this is a one-off: across all 10 reps with ≥3 deals, every other rep's count-vs-dollar gap is within ±6 points — several are even *negative* (they win their big deals more often than their small ones). Youssef's 31.4-point gap is a clear, isolated outlier (*Dollar-Weighted Win Rate by Rep*, night 2). And it's not fully absorbed by his co-rep: Polder Packaging as a whole still shows a 17.5-point gap (75.0% count vs 57.5% dollar-weighted), the second-largest of any merchant, with every other merchant within ±3 points (*Dollar-Weighted Win Rate by Merchant*, night 3). Confidence: high this is a real, isolated pattern — this is the one clean actionable lead from this whole exercise. Recommend a direct look at how he handles larger-deal negotiation/closing specifically, not his pipeline generally.

**Q2 — "Zero open pipeline" is not a freeze, it's normal volatility for a small pipeline.** Opportunity creation in Dec 2025 was low (1 new deal across all 5 merchants) but not unprecedented — monthly creation has dipped to 1-2 before (e.g., Jul 2023, Mar 2024), and the open balance has hit zero before, in Jul 2025 (*Created vs Closed by Month*, night 2; *Open Balance at Month-End*, night 1). Confidence: moderate-high. I can't fully rule out a data-freshness effect for the very last few days of December, but the pattern looks consistent with ordinary swings in a pipeline that only sees 2-9 new deals a month across all 5 merchants combined — there just isn't much volume, so empty troughs happen periodically.

**Q4 — No reliable seasonal pattern, but the sample is thin.** Lost-deal duration by quarter ranges ~18-49 days with only 1-8 lost deals per quarter — too few to call a trend (*Seasonality of Lost-Deal Cycle Time*, night 2). There's a loose hint that Q4/holiday losses ran a bit longer in 2 of 3 years, but I wouldn't act on it. Confidence: low — flagging as open, not resolved.

**Caveats:** Everything here rests on a small base (30 lost New-business deals total across 3 years, 5 merchants), so individual-quarter or individual-rep numbers can swing on a handful of deals — Youssef's result is robust because the gap is so large relative to everyone else's, but smaller gaps elsewhere shouldn't be over-read. No new instrumentation issues this week beyond the two already flagged to product (no stage-transition history, no payment-terms field for wholesaler-to-customer contracts).

**Next steps:** (1) Talk to Youssef Meijer and Polder's manager specifically about large-deal closing technique, using the €26k loss as the concrete example. (2) For the ~23% of lost deals that go quiet before dying, pull a quick list so Samuel's team can see if "kill it faster" coaching would help those specifically. (3) Q4 seasonality stays an open question — revisit only if next year's data roughly doubles the lost-deal sample.
