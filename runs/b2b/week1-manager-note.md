Note to Daniel Kim — B2B pipeline health

Daniel,

Samuel and I need to give the B2B wholesale merchants (and our own leadership) a clear-eyed view of why their sales teams are struggling to close business. This feeds a joint recommendation Samuel and I are making next week on where to invest — better pipeline coaching/tooling for merchants vs. changes to how we structure stages, quotes and terms in the product. So I want the picture to be accurate more than exhaustive — pick your battles across the nights you have.

Here's what I actually want to know, in plain terms:

1. **Where do deals die?** Across the wholesale merchants, at what point in the journey from raw lead to signed deal do we lose the most opportunities? I want to know which stage is the graveyard, not just an overall conversion number.

2. **How long does it take to close, and is that getting better or worse?** From a lead first showing up to a deal actually closing (won or lost), what's the typical cycle time, and has it been stretching out or shrinking over recent months?

3. **What separates a won deal from a lost one?** Things like deal size, how many people/touches were involved before close, which rep or team owned it, or how old the opportunity got before it closed — anything that reliably shows up more on the winning side versus the losing side.

4. **Are some merchants or reps clearly struggling more than others?** I'd like a sense of whether this is a widespread pattern across our B2B merchants or concentrated in a handful of accounts/teams, since that changes whether this is a product fix or a coaching fix.

5. (If there's room) **Does deal size or payment terms correlate with win rate or speed?** Wholesalers care a lot about credit terms — I'm curious if longer terms or larger contract values are dragging out or killing deals.

Priority order is 1, 2, 3 first — those directly shape the recommendation. 4 and 5 are valuable but can slip if you run out of slots this week.

Keep each report to one clean question — I'd rather have four sharp answers than one crowded one. Flag anything that looks like a data quality issue rather than a real trend.

Thanks — looking forward to the Friday memo.

Patrick
